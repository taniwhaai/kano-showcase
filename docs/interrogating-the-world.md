# Interrogating the world

Kano worlds are **deterministic and causal**: nothing is placed by hand. Every
feature — a ridge, a river, a desert, a stand of trees — is the consequence of
the ones before it, back to plate tectonics and a single seed. That's what
makes the world *interrogable*: you can always ask **why**, and get a real
chain of causes rather than decoration.

## Two ways to ask

### In Hīkoi (no AI needed)

Press **I** to ask about the landscape. The interrogation panel opens with six
tabs, each answering one question straight from the world:

- **Here** — what is here: elevation, climate, soil, rock, vegetation.
- **Why** — why this landscape: the tectonic and erosional story that raised it.
- **Weather** — what the sky is doing right now.
- **Ground** — what's beneath you: the stratigraphic column, plutons, gems.
- **Life** — what grows here, and why.
- **Scene** — take in the place: a first-person read of standing there.

This is Kano answering directly — the world explaining itself, and only ever
from what its own fields support.

Use the **right mouse button** to inspect a plant's species and generative
recipe. Regional location readings have a broader resolution than the local
walking surface; their elevation is not a precise height beneath your feet.

### With your AI (over MCP)

Connect Claude or another MCP client (see
[connect-your-ai.md](connect-your-ai.md)) and ask in plain language. The AI
has the full query surface and can chain many questions together — following a
river to the sea, or prospecting a whole region for where a mine would make
geological sense.

## The one principle

Nothing desirable is *placed* — each thing is the output of a requirement
chain the world ran. To find something (an ore, a gem, a particular plant),
you **invert the chain**: ask the upstream conditions, cheapest-and-broadest
first, and confirm locally only where they line up. That's real field
prospecting — province → belt → district → body — and it's why an AI can hunt
for a mine on this world and *justify* what it finds.

## Read the evidence

A causal step is reported only where the world's own fields support it. The
world reports evidence from its own fields. The viewer and MCP share the
saved world, while their queries can cover different spatial scales. An AI
client can interpret those results; inspect the underlying answers when a
claim matters.
